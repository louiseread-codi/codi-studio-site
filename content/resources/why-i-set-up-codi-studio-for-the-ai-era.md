We sat down with our founder, Louise Read, to ask why she set up Codi Studio. Louise spent 16 years as a technology marketing leader at companies including Experian, Brightpearl and Klaviyo, and the last five taking AI products to market. Before Codi Studio, she set up and ran a marketing agency with a team of ten. Today she carries a similar level of work with a team of ten AI agents behind her.

Here's the conversation, in her words.

## **What made you set up Codi Studio?**

I was going into lots of businesses, and before I started working with them, what I'd often hear is that they were using AI everywhere. The reality was very different.

Yes, they'd given licences to all their team members. Some were even providing training and AI literacy courses. They were spending a lot of money on AI. But no part of the business had been automated in a way that had an impact on the P\&L, so they weren't seeing a return.

The other thing that was really noticeable, when I worked across multiple departments, was duplicate builds. Businesses were paying twice for things they'd created. I just felt there was a better way of doing this.

## **Before Codi Studio, you ran an agency with a team of ten. What was that like?**

I was spending £30,000 to £40,000 a year on employment costs. National Insurance was increasing, and so were employer obligations. I had an office, and I encouraged the team to come in, so I ended up paying for travel stipends, lunches and team socials. Then there's maternity leave and paid sick leave. All of that adds a huge amount of cost and risk.

It would also take me a long time to hire people and train them up. That whole process was quite risky and costly for me.

## **What changed when you went deep with AI?**

When I went really deep with AI, I started to see its truly transformative power. It was able to do tasks I would have had to employ someone to do.

What I've been able to do with AI is reduce that risk, reduce the cost, and at the same time grow the business to a similar revenue number. So I'm seeing the commercial impact of AI, while reducing the costs and the risk that come with hiring, management layers and all of the complexity that comes with that.

It's also less likely to make mistakes, and it's continually learning, so it gets better and better the more time I invest in it. It has changed my entire business, and my life.

Today, Codi Studio runs on a team of ten AI agents, from a chief of staff that writes my daily brief to client managers for each account. We hit our business goals within three months.

## **What surprised you most?**

The ease of working with AI agents. You don't have the political side of things, or the challenge of people management, or the increased cost. It's enabled me to scale really quickly.

## **What went wrong at first?**

I learnt the hard way. I was experimenting with AI through building, and within about six months I had to cut a lot of what I'd built, because I wasn't seeing the impact. It was creating a lot of noise, which was increasingly exhausting.

***"I was getting a lot more output, but it wasn't necessarily good output."***

So I went back to basics. I thought through my entire business model and what I wanted to achieve with AI, and redesigned the business around that. I made sure I'd connected the right technology so AI could perform better, and gave it the right context so it would produce an output I was happy with.

There's quite a lot of work that needs to happen up front to make AI effective, as well as rethinking the power of this whole thing.

## **What do you mean by "the AI era"?**

If we zoom out, the way we do business is going to change. Intelligence is being commoditised, and the cost of intelligence is falling. A business's biggest asset always used to be its people, because that was the most costly thing. Now that we're able to translate that intelligence into an AI workforce, the whole way a business needs to operate changes.

Now that we all have access to AI, AI itself isn't the competitive advantage. The competitive advantage is being able to implement it successfully in your organisation, and get it working for you in a way that provides commercial impact. The AI era is the move from playing and dabbling with AI, and giving everyone licences, to having it fully orchestrated within your business and getting commercial value from it.

## **Why should a CEO care, rather than just buying everyone a licence?**

When people just get licences, it becomes incredibly costly for a business. You're spending on the licences, and you're spending on tokens, because people have the autonomy to build whatever they want. In theory that could be great for innovation. What we're actually seeing is duplicate builds, and a loss of control over what should and shouldn't be automated.

This has to be directed from the C-suite. If you just hand AI over to employees and expect them to figure it out, you're missing a huge step.

## **What's your vision for Codi Studio?**

***"Every business run by people and AI together, and more profitable for it."***

You still need people to point AI at the work. AI can do a lot of these tasks, but businesses still need people to decide what those tasks are and what the best bets are, and to think more strategically. Where I see this work really well is where people are orchestrating it all, working with multiple agents to deliver the work.

## **Why "more profitable", and not faster or more efficient?**

In 2026, a lot of people have just realised how powerful AI is, and the result has been a lot more output instead of revenue. More isn't always the goal.

## **Who is Codi Studio for?**

I think it's amazing for everybody. There's a huge opportunity for businesses to reduce their costs and grow with AI, regardless of size. And even at an individual level, there's a huge amount it can help with.

Every other business also has access to it, though. So you really have to design it into your business, and keep what makes you unique.

## **What do you want Codi Studio to be known for in five years?**

Helping businesses bring AI into their business, so it reduces their costs and increases their profit margins, and AI just becomes part of how they work. It's not this scary, unknown thing. They have agents in their business, they have the right governance in place, and they're seeing revenue and returns from AI.
