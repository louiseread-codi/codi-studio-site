In 2026, we saw an influx of businesses “adopting AI”, or what they thought was adopting AI. These businesses bought AI licences and ran training to get their employees using AI.

And yet, more than half of CEOs (56%) have seen no significant financial benefit from AI so far, according to [PwC’s 2026 Global CEO Survey](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-global-ceo-survey.html).

That gap is a significant opportunity for businesses that adopt AI the right way.

An AI implementation consultant’s job is to help business leaders redesign their business for the AI era, using AI to drive commercial opportunity: doubling down on automation to help you grow, or saving money with an AI workforce of agents doing the work.

In this article, we'll explore what an AI implementation consultant does, how they differ from an AI coach or an AI expert, why redesigning for AI is now a leadership decision, how to measure AI, and how to choose the right AI implementation partner.

## Why businesses need AI implementation support to see a return on AI investment

Most businesses are seeing no return on the money they've invested in AI licences, training and upskilling. The reality is that a lot of what has been built has been employee-led, with AI pointed at low-value admin tasks like drafting emails, producing content or joining up data. All of which is valuable, but none of which moves the needle on the profit and loss. If that's all a business is doing, it's just a really expensive project, and you’re not transforming anything. (We wrote more about this in [Your AI is a very expensive training pilot](https://codistudio.ai/resources/your-ai-is-a-very-expensive-training-pilot).)

What businesses actually need to do is redesign their business with AI: centralising AI builds and deployment where they will add commercial value. That's something the CEO and the leadership team need to manage, and for many CEOs it's unfamiliar territory. [Gartner found](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-20-gartner-says-44-percent-of-ceos-struggle-to-demonstrate-ai-financial-returns-as-technology-becomes-central-to-growth) that 44% of CEOs struggle to show financial returns from AI, yet 75% plan to increase their technology investment. [Anthropic's research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai), published in November 2026, found the highest-value uses of AI came from businesses that reorganised around it. When AI is simply handed to individuals, the gains stay in pockets, because individuals can't change the work beyond their own corner of the business.

## The new reality for business leaders: an AI workforce

AI continues to get more capable while its cost keeps falling. Compare that with the rising cost of employing a human workforce. Businesses that implement AI correctly can benefit in one of two ways:

* **Run a leaner business:** a reduction in workforce, fewer layers of management and lower people costs, with AI driving the growth. Cost down, profit up.
* **Maximise output and growth from the same team:** the same people, with AI on top of their work, focused on growth.

Both are valid. Businesses that buy the licences and expect things to change radically overnight won’t see returns from AI, and it will continue to be a rising cost on their P&L.

An AI implementation consultant helps you decide which route fits your business, and then makes it happen.

## What does an AI implementation consultant do?

AI consultant and strategist is now the second-fastest-growing job in the US, according to [LinkedIn’s Jobs on the Rise 2026 report](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/linkedin-jobs-rise-2026-25-fastest-growing-roles-us-linkedin-news-dlb1c). That’s no surprise, given that businesses need to start making a return on their investment. But it’s important to know which skills you’re looking for.

An AI implementation consultant helps you redesign your whole business with AI and, crucially, find where it will have a commercial impact.

An AI implementation consultant shouldn’t be confused with an AI coach or an AI expert. Those roles have very different outcomes.

An AI coach teaches employees how to use existing tools.

An AI expert builds automations and agents, but won’t necessarily tie them to commercial outcomes.

An AI implementation consultant, on the other hand, combines an understanding of business and AI. They help senior executives map out their entire business, identify processes ripe for AI and build systems and processes around them, while making sure guardrails are in place for data protection, audit trails and human approval. Crucially, an AI implementation consultant makes sure anything that is built and implemented is aligned to a clear commercial goal, like growth or money saved.

They support both the build and the implementation, rolling it out across the organisation and supporting employee enablement. They step in as an AI partner and own the entire process end to end with the CEO and the senior leadership team.

## Should you hire an AI implementation consultant or build AI in-house?

Of course, you could hire your own AI implementation consultant in-house. But as we’ve shared, this is one of the fastest-growing roles on the market right now: they’re in demand, and they will command a high salary.

Redesigning your business for the AI era is a big shift, and not one to be taken lightly. An internal person running it can come with limitations: they can get sucked into the existing way of doing things and into internal politics.

It can be really beneficial to bring in someone with an external perspective: someone who has done this for lots of different businesses, who can help you reimagine your business for the AI era, and who still has commercial acumen.

An AI implementation consultant should be able to break AI down into non-technical language for the CEO, the C-suite and the leadership team, and translate it into business outcomes, so your investment in AI has its own revenue line in your profit and loss.

That said, the right AI implementation consultant will make sure it ends up in-house anyway: your team owns and runs everything that's built.

## What an AI implementation consultant does in practice

An AI implementation consultant maps out your entire business: the departments that exist today, and the ones you wish could exist. They identify the workflows AI can take on, and tie each one to a measurable outcome, growth or money saved. That's where the value of AI gets quantified, and it becomes what the work is measured against. That's how you get more value from what you already have. AI needs three things to do real work: context, connected tools and systems.

An AI implementation consultant builds the automations and agents that do the work, centralised so you're not paying for the same build twice in different teams. ### Putting guardrails and quality control in place

An AI implementation consultant puts guardrails and quality controls in place, with humans checking processes, so the outputs are safe and reliable. That includes your data. When CEOs worry about data risk, the real question is their own data processes: whether the right employees have access to the right things, and whether there's an AI policy that puts the guardrails in place. Where a person must approve something before it goes out depends on each business's own risk appetite, and that's for the business to define. ### Training your team and handing over

An AI implementation consultant trains your team members on the real automations and agents built for your business, with clear documentation, a proper handover and internal owners for each one.

A lot of advisory companies and traditional consultants will just build, without thinking about how to roll it out across the organisation. ### Measuring and refining against commercial outcomes

An AI implementation consultant puts measurement in place, tracking against commercial outcomes: growth first, money saved second, time back last. Those outcomes are monitored over time, and the agents are refined until they deliver a good result.

## How will you know AI implementation has worked?

The answer should be simple: cost down, profit up. In practice, that looks like this:

* **AI is driving measurable growth or money saved:** the automations and agents built with your AI implementation consultant are driving measurable growth or money saved.
* **Returns:** lower costs and higher profit, with lower overheads and growth driven through automation.
* **Less risk:** less of the cost and risk that comes with employing people.
* **Faster decisions:** fewer layers of middle management, so the business is more agile.
* **AI across the organisation:** implemented in every department, beyond a few keen individuals.
* **Multiple agents doing the work:** agents conducting work across the business.
* **Orchestrators in each department:** people who decide the outcome, where to focus and what the agents do, based on data.

## How to choose an AI implementation consultant

You want an AI implementation consultant who has the technical capability and also deeply understands how businesses run. Being an expert in AI isn’t enough.

An AI implementation consultant needs to be able to translate how AI will drive value for the organisation in a way the C-suite understands and speaks the business language.

Ideally, find someone who has run a business. They'll understand the risk of employing people, how long it takes to upskill someone, and the additional costs of running a business. Very few people are looking at the bigger picture like this. A lot of AI consultants are software engineers who have worked on AI for a long time, but aren't necessarily thinking about the business as a whole.

Large advisory firms do this work too, but they're priced for large enterprises. The AI implementation consultant you choose has to fit the size of your organisation and what you can afford.

### Red flags when hiring an AI implementation consultant

* **They only talk technology.** Models, tools and prompts, with no mention of your performance indicators or the P&L. Built in isolation, AI won't change how the business performs.
* **They build and leave.** No training on what they've built, no documentation and no internal owners. Adoption stalls, and the builds stop being used.
* **Everything sits in their accounts.** If the automations and agents live in the consultant's own accounts, you'll have to keep paying them to keep it running or change anything.
* **They measure time saved or nothing at all.** Drafting emails faster isn't a commercial outcome. If there's no plan to track growth or money saved, and to refine the agents over time, you won't see it on the P&L.
* **They promise a return before they know your business.** The value is only quantified once your business has been mapped and the AI priorities are tied to measurable outcomes.
* **There are no guardrails.** Outputs going out with no human check put your data, your customers and your brand at risk.
