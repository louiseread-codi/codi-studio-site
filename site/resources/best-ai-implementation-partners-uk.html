Why trust us?

Implementing AI is our whole job. Every week we sit with leadership teams, map how their business actually runs and build AI into it with their people. That means we also see, very quickly, the moments when a client needs someone with a different specialism: a data platform that isn't ready, a security review before an agent goes live, a certification their customers are asking for.

So this is a short list: the specialists we'd point a client to for the parts of the job that sit alongside ours. We've checked every firm, person and fact on this list.

Why "AI implementation" means so many different things

Ask any leadership team whether their business uses AI and the answer is almost always the same: we use it everywhere. Look inside, and people are drafting emails faster and summarising their meetings, but very few businesses have redesigned a single workflow. What gets reported as adoption is, in practice, dabbling.

Getting from there to AI that shows up in the numbers takes several kinds of help. Someone has to plan it across the business. Someone has to get the data in order. Someone has to make sure it's safe, secure and compliant. And someone has to train the people who will use it. Very few firms do all of that well, which is why it pays to know who the specialists are.

How to choose an AI implementation partner

Start with what you're trying to change in the business, then match the specialist to the job:

  • You want AI across the business, but don't know where to start: a whole-business partner who will map your processes and build a roadmap.
  • Your data is scattered across systems: a data platform specialist.
  • You run on Microsoft 365 and want Copilot: a Microsoft partner.
  • You need hundreds of people trained: a structured training provider.
  • Customers are asking how you govern AI: a certification body.
  • AI is about to face customers or act in your systems: an AI security specialist.
  • You're in a regulated sector: a specialist who knows your rules.

Whoever you choose, ask them four questions:

  1. How will you decide what not to automate?
  2. Who in my team will own what you build?
  3. How will we measure it, beyond time saved?
  4. What happens when you leave?

Top tip from Codi Studio: measure AI in this order: growth first, money saved second, time last. An hour saved is worth nothing until something is done with it.

FAQs

What does an AI implementation partner do?

An AI implementation partner helps a business put AI to work in how it actually runs. That usually means training the team, mapping processes, deciding where AI will add value, building automations and agents, and measuring the impact. Some specialists cover all of this; others focus on one part, such as data, security or training.

How much does AI implementation cost in the UK?

It depends on the scope. A workshop for a leadership team is a short, contained piece of work. An ongoing implementation partnership is usually priced monthly, and large training programmes and enterprise data projects cost more again. A good partner will understand your business on a call before giving you a price.

Do I need an AI implementation partner, or can my team do it?

Your team can learn to use AI themselves. What's hard to do internally is redesigning how work flows across departments, because it needs someone with the time, an outside view and the authority of the leadership team behind them. That's where a partner helps.

What should I ask before hiring an AI implementation partner?

Ask how they decide what not to automate, who in your team will own what's built, how they'll measure impact beyond time saved, and what happens when the engagement ends.

What is ISO/IEC 42001?

ISO/IEC 42001 is the international standard for managing AI responsibly. Certification shows customers, insurers and regulators that a business has proper governance over how it uses AI. In the UK, BSI was the first certification body accredited by UKAS to certify it.
